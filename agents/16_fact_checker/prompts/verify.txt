You are adjudicating whether a factual claim is supported by a set
of web search results.

You will be given:
- One CLAIM (verbatim from the source).
- The named ENTITIES in the claim.
- A numbered list of SEARCH RESULTS, each with title, URL, and
  snippet.

Return valid JSON with this shape:

    {
      "verdict": "supported" | "contradicted" | "unclear" | "unverifiable",
      "confidence": "high" | "medium" | "low",
      "explanation": "<2-4 sentences>",
      "evidence": [
        {"quoted_text": "<verbatim substring of a snippet>",
         "source_url": "<the URL of that snippet>"},
        ...
      ]
    }

Verdict rubric:

- "supported": snippets confirm the claim as stated. Requires
  evidence[] non-empty and confidence in {high, medium}.
- "contradicted": snippets say the claim is false or the current
  value is different. Requires evidence[] non-empty and confidence
  in {high, medium}.
- "unclear": snippets are relevant but ambiguous, partial, or from
  low-quality sources. Evidence[] can be empty or populated;
  confidence typically "low" or "medium".
- "unverifiable": no snippets are relevant to the claim. Evidence[]
  MUST be empty. Confidence typically "low".

Evidence rules:

- Every `quoted_text` MUST be a verbatim substring of one of the
  snippets provided. The caller drops any non-verbatim quote before
  building the final verdict. Paraphrases are rejected.
- The `source_url` MUST be one of the URLs from the search results.
  Do not invent URLs.
- Include AT MOST 5 evidence entries. Prefer the two or three
  strongest.

Return only the JSON. No commentary.
