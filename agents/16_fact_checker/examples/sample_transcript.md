# A speech on landmarks, myths, and our product

Good morning everyone. I want to talk about three things today.

First, landmarks. The Eiffel Tower is located in Paris and stands
330 meters tall including its antennas. It opened to the public in
1889. These are basic facts anyone can look up, and they set the
stage for what I want to say next.

Second, myths. A myth I heard growing up is that the Great Wall of
China is visible from the Moon with the naked eye. This is one of
those claims that sounds plausible and gets repeated in schoolbooks,
but it is not true. No astronaut has ever confirmed this, and the
wall is roughly the same width as a highway seen from orbit.

Third, our own work. I believe the 2020s are the most exciting
decade for artificial intelligence, and I think this room is at the
center of it. In 2019 our internal team launched a research
prototype used by three private research groups. It never became a
product, but it taught us the shape of the technique we ship today.

I would like us to keep landmarks, myths, and our own work all
straight in our heads. Facts are cheap to check; getting them wrong
in public is expensive.
